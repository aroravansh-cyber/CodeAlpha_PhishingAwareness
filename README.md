## About the Project

**Phishing Awareness Training** is **Project #2** of my **CodeAlpha Cyber Security Internship**.

Phishing remains one of the most common ways attackers gain a foothold, because it targets people rather than systems. This project is an awareness module that teaches how to **recognize phishing emails and fake websites**, how attackers use **social engineering** to manipulate their targets, and what **practical habits** reduce the risk of falling for them. It combines explanations, real-world case studies, and interactive quizzes so learners can test what they have picked up.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this training, a learner should be able to:

- 🎣 Explain what phishing is and name its main variants
- 📧 Spot the warning signs of a phishing email
- 🌐 Tell a legitimate website from a fake one
- 🧠 Recognize the psychological tricks behind social engineering
- 🛡️ Apply everyday habits that prevent compromise
- 🚨 Know what to do after clicking something suspicious

## Red Flags at a Glance

**In an email**
- Sender address that does not match the claimed organization
- Generic greeting such as "Dear Customer"
- Pressure to act immediately ("Your account will be closed in 24 hours")
- Unexpected attachments or requests for credentials
- Links whose real destination differs from the displayed text

**On a website**
- Domain with swapped or added characters (for example `paypa1.com`)
- Login page reached through a link rather than typed or bookmarked
- Missing padlock, or a padlock on a domain that is still wrong
- Requests for far more information than the service normally needs

---

## Social Engineering Tactics Covered

| Tactic | How attackers use it |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Urgency** | Forces quick decisions before the victim can think |
| **Authority** | Poses as a boss, bank, government office, or IT team |
| **Fear** | Threatens account suspension, legal action, or data loss |
| **Curiosity / Greed** | Offers prizes, refunds, or "exclusive" content |
| **Pretexting** | Invents a believable story to extract information |
| **Baiting** | Leaves a tempting lure such as an infected USB drive |

## Real-World Examples

Case studies included in the module:

- **RSA SecurID breach (2011):** a phishing email carrying a malicious spreadsheet led to a compromise at a major security vendor.
- **Google and Facebook invoice scam (2013-2015):** a fraudster impersonated a hardware supplier and tricked both companies into paying fake invoices worth tens of millions of dollars.
- **John Podesta email compromise (2016):** a fake "security alert" email led to stolen credentials for a high-profile account.
- **Twitter account takeover (2020):** attackers used phone-based social engineering on employees to reach internal admin tools.

> Each case study breaks down what happened, which red flags were present, and what could have prevented it.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Pause before clicking, especially when a message creates urgency
- ✅ Hover over links to check the real destination
- ✅ Type the address yourself or use a bookmark for banking and email logins
- ✅ Enable **multi-factor authentication (MFA)** everywhere possible
- ✅ Use a **password manager**, which will not autofill on lookalike domains
- ✅ Verify unexpected requests through a second channel, such as a phone call to a known number
- ✅ Keep browsers, operating systems, and email filters up to date
- ✅ **Report** suspicious messages to your IT or security team instead of just deleting them
- ❌ Never share OTPs, passwords, or PINs with anyone who contacts you
